how to make an abacus

Packaging

All supplies used to make the first two abacuses

We start by putting a mark every 1.5 cm along the length of a craft stick

I like to draw straight guidelines on the same stick so I will have something to lay the dowels/rods along.

We’re making the back first, to do this we glue three craft sticks together three times, above is two craft sticks glued together along their faces

We are currently waiting for the back’s glue to set. We cut the 12’’ dowels in half, we need nine rods that are about 6’’ each.

The first two times I did this I used a box cutter, for this tutorial I wanted something less sharp, so I used the butter knife pictured

Two “sandwich” pieces made from 3 craft sticks glued together along their faces; these serve as the back of the abacus.

Next, we glue the rods to the bottom bar one at a time and let dry until we get the comb-like object on the right.

There should be enough room on the lower deck for seven beads, we are making the lower deck first.

Once the beads are placed on the rods, we apply more glue to the rods on the bottom bar and glue the rods to the middle bar

Glue on the faces of the bottom and middle bars. Apply weight and let glue dry.

A side view of the middle bar glued together. I strongly recommend applying weight when pasting the craft sticks to help the glue set and prevent the wood from warping. I suggest using an evenly-distributed weight like a block of wood, a 6lb unopened box of sparkling water seems to do the trick, previously I used 8oz rolls of quarters, but these are too easily stolen for public use, jars (containing wood beads, cat treats, etc.) are too improvisational in my opinion but seemed to work, I advise against using dumbbells.

Place the upper beads, there should be enough room in the upper deck for two beads on each rod. And glue the rods to the back. Let dry.

Apply more glue, then put on the front of the top bar and then apply weight, but try not to put weight on the beads.

We're all done!

Close to $30 for 3 handmade abacuses, enough supplies to make one more and some materials left over after that. As mentioned earlier recycled plastic or something else might be a better material. It took 4.5 rods, 12 craft sticks, 54 beads and a judicious amount of glue.

Results and leftovers

What if I want more rods on my abacus?

Nine rods is common. From the Gallery page it can be seen that you'll find frames with more rods. The standard way to get more rods is to put two abacuses next to each other.

You can make an abacus with 18 rods by glueing two smaller ones together.

With hinging tape and two smaller ones you can make a compact 18-rod frame.

For the folding version you will need these hinge pieces for the face. To make these you will cut the tape into little pieces. I used 2.5 cm with each piece of tape. I cut off the rounded edges of the sticks and taped them together with adhesive side to adhesive side.

Warning!

You can't cut hinging tape with scissors or the edges will fray, so you will need a sharp knife. Note that previously you only needed a butterknife to divide the dowels.
